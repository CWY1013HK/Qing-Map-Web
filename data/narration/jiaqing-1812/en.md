## year
The seventeenth year of the Jiaqing reign — rénshēn in the sexagenary cycle, 1812 by the Western calendar. The colophon on this screen map was cut in the first decade of the eighth lunar month of that year. On one earth, in one year, Europe and America were at war; on this painting, the Qing still drew breadth and tribute under the claim of “one family within and beyond.”

## russia
Look first to the northwest. In June 1812, Napoleon of France led the Grande Armée into Russia. As Moscow burned and winter closed in, the invasion collapsed. That European blood-trail is not the Qing’s northern frontier war — yet in world time they share the same Jiaqing year of rénshēn.

## england
Now to Britain at the western edge of the sea routes. That same year Britain and the United States went to war — the War of 1812. Ports were closed, merchant ships seized across the Atlantic. The distant storm had not yet shaken the Forbidden City; but the colophon already boasts of myriad realms arriving by ship and interpreter, tribute in tow — the pageantry of a flourishing age.

## tartary
Turn back into the map’s northwest — what Europeans once called 中屬韃靼利亞 (Chinese Tartary), the outer lands of the Manchu realm. Mongolia, Xinjiang, Shengjing, and the far northeast were dependencies and the dynasty’s own rise-lands, not the Eighteen Provinces of China proper. After Qianlong’s campaigns against the Dzungars and the reconquest of the Muslim cities, the New Frontier{mnc: (Ice Jecen)} already lay inside the unified map. By Jiaqing’s day those outer prefectures appear on the plate, no longer the omissions noted of Huang Qianren’s older 1767 chart.

## tribute
Follow the tribute roads: Korea, Annam, Ryukyu, then the southern and western sea lanes. Tributary relations were not modern borders; they braided ritual, trade, and hierarchy. When the colophon says those who offer tribute and acknowledge the throne are more than the fingers can count, it is folding maritime polities into that same imperial order.

## colophon
So read the Jiaqing-seventeen map as more than scenery. Europe had Napoleon’s ice and fire with Russia; Britain and America fought on the Atlantic; in the east, the engraver was still filling in Jinchuan, Tibet, and Xinjiang, locking dependencies and sea routes into one portable screen. One year; several worlds; meeting here.
